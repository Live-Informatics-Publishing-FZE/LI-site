  Forty-seven short stories, forty-seven doors. Behind one lies an invented city where order has long mattered more than people. Behind another is Japan, where duty is heavier than a mountain. Behind a third is a border past which reality stops being familiar.

  Dmitrii Pavlov writes with brevity and precision. Most of these stories take only a few minutes to read, but they stay with you much longer. Parable sits beside satire, everyday scenes beside the uncanny, and Pascal, Leibniz, Carroll and Brodsky join the conversation.

  The book is divided into three parts: Doors Inward, Doors to the East and Doors to the Other Side. Each story opens with an illustration by the author.

  For readers who love short fiction with hidden depths and books worth rereading.
